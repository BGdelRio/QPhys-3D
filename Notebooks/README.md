# Interactive Jupyter Notebooks

This folder contains interactive Jupyter notebooks developed within the **QPhys-3D** teaching innovation project for the *Quantum Physics* course at the **University of Valladolid (Spain)**.

The notebooks are designed for guided exploration of quantum-mechanical phenomena and can be executed online without any local installation.

## 1. Schrödinger Double-Well Interactive Lab

Interactive exploration of the quantum double-well potential, including energy eigenstates, tunnelling, localization and time evolution.

[![Open In Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/assets/colab-badge.svg)](https://colab.research.google.com/github/BGdelRio/QPhys-3D/blob/main/Notebooks/Schrodinger_Double_Well_Interactive_Lab_AI_check.ipynb)

## 2. Wave Packet Scattering: Step and Barrier

Interactive simulation of a Gaussian wave packet interacting with a potential step and a finite potential barrier. Students can explore reflection, transmission, evanescent waves and quantum tunnelling by modifying the relevant physical parameters.

[![Open In Colab](https://colab.research.google.com/assets/colab-badge.svg)](https://colab.research.google.com/github/BGdelRio/QPhys-3D/blob/main/Notebooks/Schrodinger_Wavepacket_Step_and_Barrier_Lab_AI_check.ipynb)

## How to use the notebooks

**Google Colab** is the recommended option. Click the corresponding *Open in Colab* badge to launch the notebook directly in your browser.

The notebooks can also be downloaded and executed locally in a Jupyter environment. Some interactive components require `ipywidgets`, together with the standard scientific Python libraries used in the notebooks.

## AI-assisted learning check

Each notebook includes an **AI-assisted learning check (*Comprobación asistida por IAG*)** in which students:

1. Write a short summary of what they have learned.
2. Explain a key quantum-mechanical concept in their own words.
3. Answer two conceptual verification questions.
4. Use generative AI as a critical tutor to check their reasoning rather than simply generate an answer.

Students are reminded that AI-generated feedback may contain errors and should be critically evaluated.
